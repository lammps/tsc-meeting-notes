Meeting notes:

  • TSC Governance And Leadership: Aidan explained the Technical Steering Committee’s quorum and voting rules, was elected chair with Steve recording the vote and confirmed Steve as note taker for the committee.
    • Quorum Requirements: The charter requires decisions to pass by a 50% majority of attending members, provided at least 50% of voting members are present. Aidan stated that the committee has 14 voting members, making seven attendees the quorum; approximately 10 members were present at the start, so the committee could conduct votes.
    • Chair Election: Aidan nominated themself as chair and described the role as gathering consensus from LAMMPS stakeholders and bringing decisions to the developers or TSC. Steve recorded the vote, with Aidan abstaining from voting for themself; the nomination passed with the attending voting members supporting it and no opposing vote reported (9-0-1).
    • Meeting Administration: Steve agreed to serve as note taker. The committee’s governance and charter documents are available on the LAMPS website under About, and the governance document lists the TSC members and housekeeping rules.
  • Core Developer Membership: The TSC accepted the existing LAMMPS core-developer list for the time being, discussed activity-based membership criteria, and Aidan withdrew the proposed addition of Anders Johansson pending activity review and confirmation of Anders’s interest.
    • Existing Developer List: Aidan presented the current core-developer list, including Axel Kohlmeyer, Steve Plimpton, Aidan Thompson, Richard Berger, Germaine Clavier, Joel Clemmer, Jake Gissinger, James Goff, Meg McCarthy, Stan Moore, and Trung Nguyen. The group voted on the list as a whole; Aidan reported 10 votes in favor and 1 opposing votes (10-1-0).
    • Activity Review: Axel said GitHub activity suggests three groups: highly active contributors, people doing some development or review, and people doing little or no current core development. Axel stated that core developers should be actively reviewing or programming. Aidan agreed to discuss the list with key people offline, categorize members, and bring possible removals or status changes to a future meeting; Axel agreed to provide the activity-dashboard URL.
    • Anders Nomination: Aidan proposed adding Anders Johansson, citing Anders’s work on the FLARE, Allegro, and NequIP-related model ecosystem, KOKKOS ports developed with Stan Moore, Python-based LAMMPS models using MLIAP, and work involving Cerebras hardware. Joel asked for evidence of Anders’s contributions, and Axel clarified that Anders initiated some rigid-model work while Stan completed much of it.
    • Membership Criteria: Evan asked whether contributor requirements should be formalized before proceeding. Axel explained that core developers receive write access and can approve or block pull requests, while the project needs sufficient independent review before merging. Aidan added that nominees should understand and want the commitment. After Axel shared the activity dashboard and Aidan observed that Anders appeared in the least-active category, Aidan withdrew the nomination without a vote.
    • Current Member Status: Richard said they remain willing to serve on the TSC but no longer have dedicated funding for active LAMMPS development. They plan to continue supporting specification-package maintenance and related work. Aidan confirmed that continued TSC service is separate from core-developer activity status. Richard wishes to give up core developer membership, but action on this is deferred to member ship review at the next meeting.
  • HPSF Membership And Project Governance: Aidan explained that LAMMPS joined the High Performance Software Foundation to establish institution-independent governance, described HPSF’s limited escalation role and in-kind support, and reported that legal transfer work is awaiting NNSA approval.
    • Purpose Of HPSF: Aidan explained that HPSF is part of the Linux Foundation and provides an open governance structure for widely used HPC software. LAMMPS followed the model of projects such as Kokkos and Spack, seeking to make it easier for institutions beyond Sandia to invest in LAMMPS and participate in its future.
    • Decision Authority: Day-to-day and month-to-month decisions remain with the LAMMPS TSC. HPSF would become involved mainly if project decision-making broke down or if LAMMPS no longer met the requirements for its core-stage membership; HPSF could then change LAMMPS’s membership level. The Linux Foundation would serve as the ultimate arbiter in an unresolved governance failure.
    • Support Provided: In response to Ellad’s question about funding, Aidan said HPSF does not provide direct funding. It does provide in-kind logistical support for meetings, access to experience from leaders of other open-source projects, and potentially future support for testing infrastructure.
    • Legal Transfer Status: In response to Richard’s question, Aidan said Sandia had completed its part and sent the legal package to NNSA. Approval from the NNSA head lawyer is required for transferring assets such as LAMMPS trademark rights to the Linux Foundation; Aidan said there was no known internal objection at Sandia.
    • Institutional Support Risk: Ellad asked whether transferring ownership could reduce Sandia’s incentive to support LAMMPS. Aidan acknowledged the risk but said LAMMPS had historically received bottom-up support from individual Sandia staff rather than strong top-down institutional ownership. Aidan also noted that Sandia values LAMMPS because it has demonstrated strong and weak scaling on major HPC systems.
    • In-Person Meeting: Aidan said HPSF’s annual meeting will take place in Montreal in April and confirmed that we will be holding an in-person LAMMPS TSC meeting there on Friday April 16th https://events.linuxfoundation.org/hpsf-conference/. up-to-date information about the TSC meeting at HPSF will be posted to the LAMMPS TSC Slack channel here: https://lammps.slack.com/archives/C0BQ8B5GT8T
  • CMake Transition And Makefile Retirement: Most participants reported that their organizations already use CMake and no longer depend on the legacy Make build, so the committee deferred a vote while asking Axel to define a removal plan for the next meeting.
    • Steve's Summary of entire discussion: Discussed possible deprecation of traditional make support in LAMMPS, so that CMake becomes the only option for building the code.  This will enable simplification, refactoring, and advances in how CMake works with LAMMPS. A possible downside is that legacy users of traditional make may be inconvenienced or annoyed.  Input from external TSC members was unanimous in favor of just using CMake; that is what they do themselves at their HPC centers and companies.  There appeared to be concensus to make the deprecation decision and do the deprection before the next stable release (a year from now) so that we get feedback quickly from legacy users.  However no vote was taken today.  Aidan suggested we vote on this at next TSC meeting.
    • Current Build-System Split: Aidan described the tradeoff between retaining Make for veteran users and reducing the maintenance burden of supporting two build systems. Axel clarified that Make support has already been retired for packages requiring external libraries, while Steve said Make is effectively limited to native LAMMPS code and does not support roughly 20–30 packages with external dependencies.
    • Modernization Constraints: Aidan and Richard explained that maintaining Make requires equivalent build-system changes and constrains CMake reorganizations and improvements. Axel added that contributors still need to make extra entries for legacy Make support when adding styles or packages, creating an avoidable burden even after external-library support was removed.
    • User And Organization Feedback: Nick said Oak Ridge previously had concerns but now recommends CMake to users and no longer publishes Make recipes. Christopher reported that Argonne supports CMake, Evan said NVIDIA has found the CMake backend mature for more than a year, Ian said AMD has used CMake successfully, and Tim said HPE will update the Discovery benchmark from Make to CMake.
    • Legacy Compatibility Options: Aidan asked whether the team could remove the maintenance burden while retaining a narrow legacy Make path. Richard said that would preserve the status quo and continue impeding modernization. Richard recommended making the next stable release free of the Make-based system while leaving the current stable release as a fallback for legacy users. They added that future users needing Make could generate Makefiles through CMake and adapt them as needed.
    • Removal Approach: Axel said the project has already completed the compatibility-preserving steps and that the next step would be removal. A conservative approach would remove Make, avoid breaking changes or major CMake rewrites until after the next stable release, and use feedback from users who report a problem. Steve supported removing Make from future feature releases, noting that the project may not know the true number of legacy users until the change occurs.
    • Deferred Decision: Ellad recommended soliciting community feedback because CMake-version differences had caused issues for the OpenKIM integration. Aidan noted that users often respond only after encountering a problem. No vote was held; Aidan proposed a shorter discussion at the next meeting, with Axel preparing the proposed future without Make, followed by a vote.
  • Quarterly Meeting Schedule: Aidan confirmed that the TSC will meet quarterly, with the next meeting planned for January and another planned for April, replacing the regular LAMMPS developer meeting schedule.
    • Meeting Cadence: In response to Steve’s question, Aidan said TSC meetings will occur four times per year, generally in January, April, July, and October, and will replace the regular LAMMPS developer meetings (which will continue to be held monthly, minus the quaterly TSC meetings)
